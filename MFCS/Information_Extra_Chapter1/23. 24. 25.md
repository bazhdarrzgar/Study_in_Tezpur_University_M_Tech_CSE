# 23, 24, & 25. Matrix Inverse, Identity Matrix, and Solving $Ax = b$

This guide covers three connected foundational concepts:
1. **Matrix inverse ($A^{-1}$)**
2. **Identity matrix ($I$)**
3. **Why the inverse solves $Ax=b$**

---

# 23. Matrix Inverse ($A^{-1}$)

Think about normal numbers first:

$$
5 \times \frac{1}{5} = 1
$$

Here, $\frac{1}{5}$ is the **multiplicative inverse of $5$** because multiplying by it gives $1$.

### For matrices, it is the exact same idea:

Instead of $5 \times \frac{1}{5} = 1$, we have:

$$
\boxed{A A^{-1} = I}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{A^{-1} A = I}
$$

where $I$ is the **identity matrix** (the matrix equivalent of $1$).

---

# 24. Identity Matrix ($I$)

The identity matrix $I$ acts as the number **$1$** for matrix multiplication:

$$
I =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

For any compatible matrix $A$:

$$
\boxed{A I = A} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \boxed{I A = A}
$$

### Verification Example with Calculations:

Let:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

#### 1. Calculate $AI$:

$$
AI =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

* **Row 1 × Col 1:** $2(1) + 3(0) = 2 + 0 = 2$
* **Row 1 × Col 2:** $2(0) + 3(1) = 0 + 3 = 3$
* **Row 2 × Col 1:** $4(1) + 5(0) = 4 + 0 = 4$
* **Row 2 × Col 2:** $4(0) + 5(1) = 0 + 5 = 5$

$$
AI =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
= A \quad \checkmark
$$

#### 2. Calculate $IA$:

$$
IA =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

* **Row 1 × Col 1:** $1(2) + 0(4) = 2 + 0 = 2$
* **Row 1 × Col 2:** $1(3) + 0(5) = 3 + 0 = 3$
* **Row 2 × Col 1:** $0(2) + 1(4) = 0 + 4 = 4$
* **Row 2 × Col 2:** $0(3) + 1(5) = 0 + 5 = 5$

$$
IA =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
= A \quad \checkmark
$$

Multiplying by $I$ on either side leaves the matrix unchanged!

---

# 25. Why is the Inverse Useful? ($x = A^{-1}b$)

Suppose we want to solve a linear system:

$$
\boxed{Ax = b}
$$

In normal algebra, if you have $5x = 10$, you isolate $x$ by multiplying by the inverse $\frac{1}{5}$:

$$
x = \frac{1}{5}(10) = 2
$$

With matrices, you **cannot divide by a matrix**. Instead, you multiply both sides on the left by $A^{-1}$:

$$
A^{-1}(Ax) = A^{-1}b
$$

Regroup using associativity:

$$
(A^{-1}A)x = A^{-1}b
$$

Because $A^{-1}A = I$:

$$
Ix = A^{-1}b
$$

And because $Ix = x$:

$$
\boxed{x = A^{-1}b}
$$

This gives an explicit, exact formula for the solution vector $x$!

---

## Complete Worked Example

Let:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
b =
\begin{bmatrix}
4 \\
9
\end{bmatrix}
$$

We want to solve:

$$
Ax = b \implies
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
x_1 \\
x_2
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
4 \\
9
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Step 1: Find the inverse $A^{-1}$
For a diagonal matrix, invert each diagonal element:

$$
A^{-1} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{3}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Quick check that $A A^{-1} = I$:**
$$
A A^{-1} =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{3}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
2(\frac{1}{2}) + 0 & 0 + 0 \\
0 + 0 & 3(\frac{1}{3})
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
= I \quad \checkmark
$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate $x = A^{-1}b$

$$
x =
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{3}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
4 \\
9
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Middle calculations:**
* **Row 1:** $\frac{1}{2}(4) + 0(9) = 2 + 0 = 2$
* **Row 2:** $0(4) + \frac{1}{3}(9) = 0 + 3 = 3$

So:

$$
\boxed{x =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
3
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

---

### Step 3: Verify the Solution ($Ax = b$)

Multiply $A$ by our solution $x$:

$$
Ax =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
3
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
2(2) + 0(3) \\
0(2) + 3(3)
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
4 \\
9
\end{bmatrix}
= b \quad \checkmark
$$

The solution is verified!

---

# 🧠 Core Summary

| Concept | Matrix Rule | Ordinary Number Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Identity** | $I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ | The number $1$ |
| **Identity Property** | $AI = IA = A$ | $5 \times 1 = 5$ |
| **Inverse Definition** | $A A^{-1} = A^{-1} A = I$ | $5 \times \frac{1}{5} = 1$ |
| **Solving System** | $Ax = b \implies \mathbf{x = A^{-1}b}$ | $5x = 10 \implies x = \frac{1}{5}(10) = 2$ |

> **Invertibility Condition:** $A^{-1}$ only exists for square matrices with non-zero pivots (non-zero determinant).
