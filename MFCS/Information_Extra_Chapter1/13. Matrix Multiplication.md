# 13. Matrix Multiplication (Row × Column Rule)

To multiply two matrices $A$ and $B$, use the **Row $\times$ Column rule**:

> **Rule:** Each entry $(i, j)$ in the product matrix $AB$ is computed by taking **Row $i$ of $A$** and **Column $j$ of $B$**, multiplying corresponding numbers, and adding them up.

---

## Example

Let:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix} \quad (2 \times 2),
\qquad
B =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & 3 \\
4 & 5 & 6
\end{bmatrix} \quad (2 \times 3)
$$

### 1. Dimension Check:
* Size of $A$: $2 \times \mathbf{2}$
* Size of $B$: $\mathbf{2} \times 3$
* The inner dimensions match ($2 = 2$).
* The resulting matrix $AB$ will be **$2 \times 3$** (with $2$ rows and $3$ columns, total $6$ entries).

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Middle Calculations

$$
AB =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
0 & 3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & 3 \\
4 & 5 & 6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Row 1 of $A = [1 \quad 2]$:

* **Entry $(1, 1)$ [Row 1 of $A$ × Column 1 of $B$]:**
  $$[1 \quad 2] \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = 1(1) + 2(4) = 1 + 8 = 9$$

* **Entry $(1, 2)$ [Row 1 of $A$ × Column 2 of $B$]:**
  $$[1 \quad 2] \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = 1(2) + 2(5) = 2 + 10 = 12$$

* **Entry $(1, 3)$ [Row 1 of $A$ × Column 3 of $B$]:**
  $$[1 \quad 2] \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} = 1(3) + 2(6) = 3 + 12 = 15$$

---

### Row 2 of $A = [0 \quad 3]$:

* **Entry $(2, 1)$ [Row 2 of $A$ × Column 1 of $B$]:**
  $$[0 \quad 3] \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = 0(1) + 3(4) = 0 + 12 = 12$$

* **Entry $(2, 2)$ [Row 2 of $A$ × Column 2 of $B$]:**
  $$[0 \quad 3] \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = 0(2) + 3(5) = 0 + 15 = 15$$

* **Entry $(2, 3)$ [Row 2 of $A$ × Column 3 of $B$]:**
  $$[0 \quad 3] \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} = 0(3) + 3(6) = 0 + 18 = 18$$

---

## 3. Final Result Matrix

Assemble all 6 calculated entries into their respective positions:

$$
\boxed{AB =
\begin{bmatrix}
9 & 12 & 15 \\
12 & 15 & 18
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

---

## 4. The Column Perspective (Alternative View)

Each column of $AB$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$:

* **Column 1 of $AB$:**
  $$1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 4 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix}$$

* **Column 2 of $AB$:**
  $$2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 5 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 15 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 12 \\ 15 \end{bmatrix}$$

* **Column 3 of $AB$:**
  $$3 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 6 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 12 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 15 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix}$$

Both the Row $\times$ Column method and the Column perspective give the exact same matrix!
