# 27. Gauss-Jordan Method

Let's start with a simple matrix:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Gaussian elimination

The goal is only to make the **bottom-left** numbers zero:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

To eliminate the $3$ in row 2, we subtract $3$ times row 1 from row 2:

$$
R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 2:**
* First entry: $3 - 3(1) = 3 - 3 = 0$
* Second entry: $4 - 3(2) = 4 - 6 = -2$

Row 1 stays unchanged: $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$.

So the matrix becomes:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
0&-2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This is called **upper triangular form** (all entries below the main diagonal are $0$).

---

### Gauss-Jordan

Gauss-Jordan goes further.

We want to reach the identity matrix:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

So we eliminate **both below AND above** the pivots.

Start with where Gaussian elimination left off:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
0&-2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

#### Step 1: Make the second pivot $1$

Divide row 2 by $-2$ (or multiply by $-\frac{1}{2}$):

$$
R_2\leftarrow-\frac{1}{2}R_2
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 2:**
* First entry: $-\frac{1}{2}\times 0 = 0$
* Second entry: $-\frac{1}{2}\times(-2) = 1$

Matrix becomes:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

#### Step 2: Remove the $2$ above the pivot

Subtract $2$ times row 2 from row 1:

$$
R_1\leftarrow R_1-2R_2
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 1:**
* First entry: $1 - 2(0) = 1 - 0 = 1$
* Second entry: $2 - 2(1) = 2 - 2 = 0$

Row 2 stays unchanged: $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.

Matrix becomes:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

That's Gauss-Jordan!

### Remember:

**Gaussian:**

$$
A\rightarrow U
$$

Usually stops at:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
*&*&*\\
0&*&*\\
0&0&*
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Gauss-Jordan:**

$$
A\rightarrow I
$$

# 28. Finding $A^{-1}$

This is where Gauss-Jordan becomes **very useful**.

Suppose:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Put $A$ next to $I$:

$$
[A \mid I]
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\left[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
1&2&1&0\\
3&4&0&1
\end{array}
\right]}
$$

Now perform row operations.

Your goal is:

$$
\boxed{
[A \mid I]\rightarrow[I \mid A^{-1}]}
$$

### Step-by-step row reduction of $[A \mid I]$:

Start with the augmented matrix:

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
1&2&1&0\\
3&4&0&1
\end{array}
\right]
$$

#### Step 1: Eliminate below the first pivot (make entry $(2,1)$ equal to $0$)

Row operation:
$$
R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 2 (element by element):**
* Column 1: $3 - 3(1) = 3 - 3 = 0$
* Column 2: $4 - 3(2) = 4 - 6 = -2$
* Column 3: $0 - 3(1) = 0 - 3 = -3$
* Column 4: $1 - 3(0) = 1 - 0 = 1$

Matrix becomes:

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
1&2&1&0\\
0&-2&-3&1
\end{array}
\right]
$$

#### Step 2: Make the second pivot equal to $1$

Row operation:
$$
R_2 \leftarrow -\frac{1}{2}R_2
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 2 (element by element):**
* Column 1: $-\frac{1}{2} \times 0 = 0$
* Column 2: $-\frac{1}{2} \times (-2) = 1$
* Column 3: $-\frac{1}{2} \times (-3) = \frac{3}{2}$
* Column 4: $-\frac{1}{2} \times 1 = -\frac{1}{2}$

Matrix becomes:

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
1&2&1&0\\
0&1&\frac{3}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}
\end{array}
\right]
$$

#### Step 3: Eliminate above the second pivot (make entry $(1,2)$ equal to $0$)

Row operation:
$$
R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2
$$

**Middle calculations for Row 1 (element by element):**
* Column 1: $1 - 2(0) = 1 - 0 = 1$
* Column 2: $2 - 2(1) = 2 - 2 = 0$
* Column 3: $1 - 2\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) = 1 - 3 = -2$
* Column 4: $0 - 2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 0 - (-1) = 0 + 1 = 1$

Matrix becomes:

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
1&0&-2&1\\
0&1&\frac{3}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}
\end{array}
\right]
$$

---

The left side is now the identity matrix $I$:

$$
[I \mid A^{-1}]
$$

Therefore, the right side is our inverse matrix $A^{-1}$:

$$
\boxed{
A^{-1}=
\begin{bmatrix}
-2&1\\
\frac{3}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

### Quick Check: Does $A A^{-1} = I$?

Let's multiply $A$ and $A^{-1}$ to verify:

$$
A A^{-1} =
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
-2&1\\
\frac{3}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

* **Row 1 × Col 1:** $(1)(-2) + (2)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) = -2 + 3 = 1$
* **Row 1 × Col 2:** $(1)(1) + (2)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 1 - 1 = 0$
* **Row 2 × Col 1:** $(3)(-2) + (4)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) = -6 + 6 = 0$
* **Row 2 × Col 2:** $(3)(1) + (4)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 3 - 2 = 1$

$$
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}
= I \quad \checkmark
$$

### ⭐ Memorize:

$$
\boxed{[A \mid I]\rightarrow[I \mid A^{-1}]}
$$

**Left becomes $I$ → right becomes $A^{-1}$.**

# 29. Transpose $A^T$

Transpose is **much easier**.

It simply means:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rows become columns}}
$$

Example:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&4\\
0&3&5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This is $2\times3$.

After transpose:

$$
A^T=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
2&3\\
4&5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now it is $3\times2$.

---

### Look at it visually:

There are two equally valid ways to think about this:

#### Way 1: Rows of $A$ become Columns of $A^T$

* **Row 1 of $A$:**
  $$
  \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}
  $$
* **Row 2 of $A$:**
  $$
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}
  $$

Putting the two columns side-by-side gives:

$$
A^T =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
2 & 3\\
4 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

#### Way 2: Columns of $A$ become Rows of $A^T$

* **Column 1 of $A$:**
  $$
  \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
  $$
* **Column 2 of $A$:**
  $$
  \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
  $$
* **Column 3 of $A$:**
  $$
  \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}
  $$

Stacking the three rows gives:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&4\\
0&3&5
\end{bmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\text{Transpose}}
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
2&3\\
4&5
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

# 30. Easy visual trick

Imagine folding the matrix along this diagonal:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{bmatrix}
\color{red}{1}&2&4\\
0&\color{red}{3}&5
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

The diagonal stays where it is, while everything else switches sides.

# 🧠 Final cheat sheet

| Concept              | What to remember                     |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Gaussian elimination | $A\rightarrow U$                     |
| Gauss-Jordan         | $A\rightarrow I$                     |
| Find inverse         | $[A \mid I]\rightarrow[I \mid A^{-1}]$ |
| Transpose            | Rows ↔ Columns                       |
| Transpose size       | $m\times n\rightarrow n\times m$     |

### Most important:

$$
\boxed{\text{Gauss-Jordan = keep doing row operations until you get }I}
$$

$$
\boxed{[A \mid I]\rightarrow[I \mid A^{-1}]}
$$

$$
\boxed{A^T=\text{rows and columns switched}}
$$
