# 18. Permutation Matrices ($P$)

A **permutation matrix** $P$ is an identity matrix with its rows rearranged. When multiplied by another matrix, it systematically reorders the rows or columns.

---

## 1. What is a Permutation Matrix?

Start with the $2 \times 2$ identity matrix:

$$
I =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Swap Row 1 and Row 2 of $I$:

$$
P =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This permutation matrix $P$ represents the operation:

$$
\boxed{\text{Swap Row 1 and Row 2 } (R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2)}
$$

---

## 2. General Algebraic Demonstration ($PA$)

Let:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Multiply on the **left** by $P$:

$$
PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Middle Calculations:
* **Row 1:**
  * Entry $(1, 1)$: $0(a) + 1(c) = c$
  * Entry $(1, 2)$: $0(b) + 1(d) = d$
* **Row 2:**
  * Entry $(2, 1)$: $1(a) + 0(c) = a$
  * Entry $(2, 2)$: $1(b) + 0(d) = b$

$$
\boxed{PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
c & d \\
a & b
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

The rows have successfully swapped!

---

## 3. Concrete Numerical Example

Let:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Multiply by $P$ on the left:

$$
PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Middle Calculations:
* **Entry $(1, 1)$:** $0(1) + 1(3) = 0 + 3 = 3$
* **Entry $(1, 2)$:** $0(2) + 1(4) = 0 + 4 = 4$
* **Entry $(2, 1)$:** $1(1) + 0(3) = 1 + 0 = 1$
* **Entry $(2, 2)$:** $1(2) + 0(4) = 2 + 0 = 2$

$$
\boxed{PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
3 & 4 \\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

So:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\text{Multiply by } P}
\begin{bmatrix}
3 & 4 \\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 4. Why Does This Work? (Linear Combination View)

Look at each row of $P$:

$$
P =
\begin{bmatrix}
\color{red}{0} & \color{blue}{1} \\
\color{blue}{1} & \color{red}{0}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

* **Row 1 of $P$:** $[0 \quad 1]$ means:
  $$0 \times (\text{Row 1 of } A) + 1 \times (\text{Row 2 of } A) = \text{Row 2 of } A$$
  *(The new Row 1 is the old Row 2).*

* **Row 2 of $P$:** $[1 \quad 0]$ means:
  $$1 \times (\text{Row 1 of } A) + 0 \times (\text{Row 2 of } A) = \text{Row 1 of } A$$
  *(The new Row 2 is the old Row 1).*

---

## 5. Permutation with 3 Rows ($3 \times 2$)

Suppose:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4 \\
5 & 6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

We want to swap **Row 1 and Row 3** ($R_1 \leftrightarrow R_3$), leaving Row 2 unchanged.

Construct $P$ by swapping Row 1 and Row 3 of the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix:

$$
P =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Multiply on the left ($PA$):

$$
PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4 \\
5 & 6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Middle Calculations:
* **Row 1:**
  * Col 1: $0(1) + 0(3) + 1(5) = 5$
  * Col 2: $0(2) + 0(4) + 1(6) = 6$
* **Row 2:**
  * Col 1: $0(1) + 1(3) + 0(5) = 3$
  * Col 2: $0(2) + 1(4) + 0(6) = 4$
* **Row 3:**
  * Col 1: $1(1) + 0(3) + 0(5) = 1$
  * Col 2: $1(2) + 0(4) + 0(6) = 2$

$$
\boxed{PA =
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 6 \\
3 & 4 \\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}}
$$

Rows 1 and 3 are successfully swapped!

---

## 6. Special Mathematical Properties of $P$

1. **Orthogonal:** The transpose equals the inverse:
   $$\boxed{P^T = P^{-1}}$$
2. **Self-inverse for single swaps:** Swapping the same two rows twice puts them back in original order:
   $$\boxed{P^2 = I \implies P = P^{-1}}$$

---

## ⭐ Key Takeaway

* **$P$ on the LEFT ($PA$):** Rearranges **ROWS**
* **$P$ on the RIGHT ($AP$):** Rearranges **COLUMNS**
